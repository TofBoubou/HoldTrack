Abstract

In DanceTrack, dancers look alike and touch each other often as they change formation. For a strong tracker built on SAM 3, about three quarters of the HOTA that oracle corrections recover is lost when people are in contact, through misplaced boxes, identity switches and missed persons. At each contact we decide which person keeps an identity and where the box of a partly occluded person ends, from several models run over the whole video. For identities, a chain of 21 steps combines cues that err differently: faces, appearance, position and tracker passes that differ in memory update, in temporal direction or by mirroring. Passes share errors, yet a forward and a backward pass that both err pick the same wrong person less than half as often as two forward ones. The chain changes an identity when two cues agree, or on one cue under a condition on the identities, and includes a learned link classifier. DanceTrack annotates the full extent of each person, and a small corrector learns it from the outputs of fine-tuned detectors, frozen pose estimators and a body-mesh model, without fine-tuning the tracker. On 57 annotated videos, HoldTrack gives 96% of matched boxes their person's main identity. Run offline, it scores 89.4 HOTA on the DanceTrack test set, 9.8 points above the best published tracker. On MOT17, with its steps selected again, it scores 68.9 HOTA, the highest among published trackers. The box decision adds 1.6 points to a base system that already ranks above every published tracker, the identity decision 3.2, and both 4.8.
